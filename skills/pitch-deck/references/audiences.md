# Audiences and outlines

One deck per audience. They share a core and differ at the edges.

| | Investor | Customer | Partner or enterprise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Decision | Take the meeting, then invest | Start using it, or buy | Run a pilot, integrate, distribute |
| They buy | The company's future return | An outcome for their own work | A gap closed or growth for their users |
| Opens with | Purpose, the problem, the insight | Their week, in their words | Their goal and their users |
| Proof | Traction, retention, market maths | Features shown working | Fit, integration path, pilot results |
| Closes with | The ask and its milestone | Start free, or book a demo | A pilot proposal with dates |
| Drop | Feature tours, pricing tables | Market size, cap tables | Fundraising, consumer onboarding |

**Shared core** (reuse across all three): the problem, what the product does today, how it
works, and why it compounds. Write these once, then build each audience's opening and close
around them.

## Investor deck (about 14 slides)

Each slide must answer the investor's question for it.

| # | Slide | The question it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cover | What is this, in one line? |
| 2 | Purpose | Why does this company exist? One sentence. |
| 3 | Problem | Whose pain, how often, and what do they do today? |
| 4 | Insight | What do you know that others do not? |
| 5 | Solution | What does it do, and which parts are live? |
| 6 | Product today | Is it real? Show it (a live demo beats a table). |
| 7 | Why it compounds | Why do users stay and grow? The flywheel. |
| 8 | Why now | What changed that makes this possible or urgent now? |
| 9 | Market | How big, sized bottom-up: users times what they pay? |
| 10 | Competition | Who else, and why will you win? |
| 11 | Business model | How do you make money, and how does it grow per user? |
| 12 | Traction | What is the evidence it works? Dated numbers. |
| 13 | Vision | Where does this go if it works? Labelled as future. |
| 14 | Team | Why are you the people to build it? |
| 15 | The ask | How much, for which milestone, by when? |

Notes that matter here:

- **Market:** show the arithmetic (number of buyers, revenue each, share you can win) and
  cite every input. Top-down analyst totals do not persuade.
- **Traction:** every figure dated and pulled from the system of record, with the query
  saved. Note known data caveats in the speaker notes before someone else finds them.
- **The thesis metric:** find the one number that proves the core claim (for example,
  retention of users who use two parts of the product versus one) and give it a tile.
- **The ask** buys a checkable milestone that sets up the next round. Tie its target to the
  market slide.

## Customer deck (about 16 slides)

| Part | Slides |
| --- | --- |
| Open | Cover; their situation (a recognisable week, not drama); who it is for (cards per audience segment, each with its entry task); the map of what the product does |
| Features | One slide per job they will gladly use right away, ordered by how often they hit that job. Situation, verified answer, next action. Animate the two or three flows that are easier to show than to tell. |
| Close | The record or layer that ties it together; how to start in three steps; one action |

- Lead each feature slide with the **hook**: the single capability someone would switch for
  (no install, no platform fee, payment held until delivery). Then the supporting points.
- Put limits in the footer or the notes in plain words, not in a hidden asterisk.
- The resolution line of the "situation" slide states the **founder's thesis**, not just the
  absence of pain. Ask for it if unsure (see questions.md).
- No numbers are needed, but prices and fees are pointed to their live page, not quoted
  from memory.

## Partner or enterprise deck (about 10 slides)

1. Cover naming both parties.
2. Their goal, in their words.
3. Who our users are and what they do (only verified facts).
4. What the partnership gives their users or business.
5. How it works: the integration or distribution path, with what exists today.
6. Proof: pilots, usage, case material you have permission to share.
7. Security, data and compliance answers they will ask about.
8. The pilot proposal: scope, success measure, timeline, owners.
9. Commercials, if ready; otherwise the shape of them.
10. Contacts and next step.

Ask what kind of partner it is (a payments provider, a platform that would embed you, a
distributor, a large customer) before writing: the argument changes completely.

## Short forms

- **Demo day or one minute:** cover, problem, product, traction, ask. Five slides, one line
  each.
- **Teaser (sent cold):** eight to ten slides that read without a speaker. Headlines carry
  the full argument; notes are not seen.
- **Appendix:** detailed metrics, roadmap, team bios. Keep the main deck lean and point here.
